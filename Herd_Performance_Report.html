  • Active Breeding Capacity: The breeding flock currently stands at 81 total ewes (50 open/lactating ewes + 31 confirmed pregnant ewes).

4. Operational & Financial Risk Evaluation

Biological vs. Administrative Performance Gap: While the flock demonstrates excellent biological productivity, low mortality (2.7%), and high slaughter throughput (40 head), the 37 missing active head (21.9% of active inventory) presents three immediate operational and financial risks:

  • 1. Feed Ration & Expense Misallocation: Formulating daily feed rations for 170 active system head when only 133 animals are present causes a ~22% overestimation in feed budgeting, leading to financial waste.
  • 2. Asset Overvaluation: Balance sheets overstate live biological assets by 37 head until a physical write-off or locate operation is finalized.
  • 3. Breeding Schedule Confusion: Unverified ewes listed as active/pregnant distort ram-to-ewe breeding group ratios for the upcoming season.

5. Recommended Immediate Action Plan & Next Steps

  • Step 1 (Physical Search): Conduct a targeted physical pen audit focused on the 16 missing male lambs and 9 missing ewes.
  • Step 2 (Feed Adjustment): Immediately adjust daily feed ration calculations to match the 133 physically verified head.
  • Step 3 (Tag Linking): Standardize ear-tag replacement logs to eliminate duplicate tags (347, 171, 101) and prevent recycled tag confusion.
  • Step 4 (Breeding Targets): Finalize breeding pen allocations for the 31 pregnant ewes entering fall lambing.
